Sizing is the decision people most often get wrong, and it is the one that shows. A box
with too much headroom lets the burger slide and arrive leaning against one wall, with
the sauce on the cardboard rather than in the bun. A box with too little presses the lid
onto the crown of the bun, flattens it, and pushes the fillings out the side.

## Measure the build, not the burger

Measure your tallest regular build, assembled, with the crown on, and take three
figures: length, width and height. Do it with the item you actually sell most of rather
than the biggest thing on the menu, and do it after the burger has sat for a minute —
a freshly stacked build settles.

Then add a little. A few millimeters of clearance on each side is enough to get the
burger in and out without smearing it against the wall, and a few on top stops the lid
touching the bun. More than that and you are paying for air and letting the build move.

## Where wrapping changes the answer

If you wrap the burger in paper before boxing it, measure it wrapped. The wrap adds
bulk and changes how the burger sits, and it is the most common reason a box that
measured correctly turns out to be tight in service.

## Standard sizes as a starting point

Most orders start from a standard size and adjust. Sliders need a shallow, small
footprint; single burgers are the common middle; tall boxes exist for stacked builds
where height is the constraint rather than width; doubles and large formats handle
either a bigger burger or a burger with a side in the same box. Browse the range under
[burger boxes by size](/product-category/boxes-by-size/) and treat the nearest one as a
baseline.

## When to go custom instead

Custom dimensions are worth it when your build is genuinely an odd shape, when you want
a burger and a side in one box without a divider rattling around, or when your volume
is high enough that a slightly smaller box saves real money on board. Because every run
here is printed to order anyway, cutting to your own dimensions costs no more in setup
than taking a standard size.

## Sizing for delivery

Delivery adds a constraint that dine-in does not: boxes get stacked, tilted and carried.
For delivery-heavy operations the box wants to be snug rather than generous, and a
sturdier board matters more than it does over the counter — the trade-offs are set out
under [burger boxes by material](/product-category/boxes-by-material/).
