A box that works perfectly over the counter can fail badly on a twenty-minute delivery, and
almost always for the same four reasons. None of them is about the burger.

## Time

Everything gets worse with time. Grease that has not reached the board in two minutes will
have reached it in fifteen. Steam that has not softened the bun over the counter will have
softened it by the time the rider arrives. Specify for the worst realistic journey, not the
average one.

## Stacking

Delivery boxes do not travel alone. They go into a bag with other orders, and the bottom one
carries everything above it. That is a compression load a single layer of cardboard is not
designed for, which is why corrugated board — with its stiffening fluted layer — is the
default for delivery-heavy operations. The comparison is set out under
[burger boxes by material](/product-category/boxes-by-material/).

## Movement

A burger that fits loosely will slide, and a burger that slides arrives leaning against one
wall with the sauce on the board. This is the most common delivery complaint and the
cheapest to fix: size the box to the build with only a few millimeters of clearance, and use
an insert where the build is unstable. The method is in our guide to
[choosing a burger box size](/how-to-choose-a-burger-box-size/).

## Steam

A sealed box keeps heat in and steam in, and steam is what turns a crisp bun soft and a
crisp fry limp. Ventilation lets it out but lets heat out with it, which is a genuine
trade-off on a long journey rather than a problem with an obvious answer.

The practical resolution is to stop treating the order as one thing. Vent what should stay
crisp and seal what should stay hot: fries in a vented box, the burger in a closed one. One
compromise box does neither job well.

## Splitting the order

That principle extends across the whole order. Fries in with the burger will steam it, sauce
loose in the bag will leak, and a drink alongside an unsupported box will crush it. Fries
boxes, sauce pots with lids and delivery bags all exist to keep those items apart, and they
print on the same run in the same artwork — the range is under
[food packaging for burger shops](/product-category/complementary-food-packaging/).

## Grease, specifically

On a long journey a coating alone often is not enough, and the fix is a barrier inside the
box rather than a heavier box: a liner under the build, or a wrap around the burger itself.
It costs less than upgrading the board and works better, because it addresses the grease
where it starts — the options are compared in our guide to
[greaseproof burger packaging](/greaseproof-burger-packaging/).

## A delivery specification, in short

Corrugated board for the stack. Snug sizing with an insert if the build is tall. Vented
packaging for anything fried, sealed for anything that must stay hot. A liner or wrap for
grease. Separate containers for fries, sauces and drinks.
