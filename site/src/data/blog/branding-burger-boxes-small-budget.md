Branded packaging does not have to mean four-color artwork across every face. Most of the
effect comes from a few cheap decisions, and the expensive ones are easier to justify once
the design has proven itself.

## Start with the stock, not the print

The single cheapest branding decision is the color of the board. Brown kraft reads as plain
and deliberate; black reads as premium; white is a blank canvas that makes any print look
sharper. Choosing a stock color costs nothing extra on the run and changes how the box
reads before a single mark is printed. The options are under
[burger boxes by color](/product-category/boxes-by-color/).

## One color goes a long way

A single-color logo, printed well and placed with space around it, almost always looks
better than full-color artwork spread across the box. It is cheaper to run, it reproduces
more consistently on uncoated board, and it ages better — you will not want to reprint it in
six months.

If you only do one thing, do this: one mark, one color, plenty of empty board around it.

## Sleeves and stickers

Both let you brand a plain box without printing the box. A sleeve wraps the box and carries
the artwork, so a single plain box works across several products with different sleeves. A
sticker does the same job for less, and can be changed for a season or a promotion without
touching the packaging order.

They are the right answer when you sell several items and cannot justify a printed run for
each — the range is under
[custom printed burger packaging](/product-category/boxes-by-customization/).

## Consistency beats coverage

A customer notices whether the fries box, the sauce pot and the burger box look like they
came from the same place. They do not notice whether the artwork covers eighty percent of
the box or twenty. Ordering the whole order's packaging on one run in one artwork is the
cheapest way to look coherent — see
[food packaging for burger shops](/product-category/complementary-food-packaging/).

## Where to spend when you can

Once the design has settled and you know it is not changing, spot UV on a single element is
the most visible upgrade per dollar. It is a gloss layer over selected artwork, so a logo
catches the light against a matte background — the trade-offs are set out in our guide to
[choosing a finish for printed boxes](/gloss-matte-or-spot-uv-burger-boxes/).

## What not to spend on first

Full-bleed photography on packaging is expensive to run and dates fast. Multiple bespoke box
shapes cost more than the effect is worth early on. And a window costs structure for a
benefit most delivery orders never see.
