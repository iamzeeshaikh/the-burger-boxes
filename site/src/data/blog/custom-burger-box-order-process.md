Custom packaging puts people off because the process is opaque — it is not obvious what you
are supposed to provide, or what you are committing to when. It is simpler than it looks.

## What a quote actually needs

Three things. The internal dimensions the box has to hold, given as length, width and height
rather than as a description. The style and board you want. And the quantity, or a range if
you are still deciding.

That is enough for a real price. Artwork is not required to start, which is the part most
people do not realize — you can get a quote before you have a design.

## If you do not know the size

Measure your tallest regular build, assembled, with the crown on, and send those three
figures. If you wrap the burger before boxing it, measure it wrapped. If you would rather
start from something standard and adjust, pick the nearest from
[burger boxes by size](/product-category/boxes-by-size/) and say what is wrong with it.

The full method, including the clearance to leave, is in our guide to
[choosing a burger box size](/how-to-choose-a-burger-box-size/).

## Artwork

Design work is prepared for you at no charge, so a logo and a rough idea of the look are
enough to begin. Vector files reproduce best; a reasonably large image will usually work.
You will see the layout before anything is printed, and that is the point to change your
mind — not after.

If you have no logo yet, colored stock with no print at all is a perfectly reasonable first
order, and print can be added on the reorder. The options are under
[burger boxes by color](/product-category/boxes-by-color/).

## Why the price on a product page is a starting point

Every run is printed to order rather than picked from stock. Setting up a press costs the
same whether it produces a hundred boxes or a hundred thousand, so that setup is spread
across the run — which is why the unit price falls as the quantity rises, and why a figure on
a product page can only ever be where pricing starts.

It is also why there is a minimum. Below a certain run length the setup dominates the price
and the boxes cost more per unit than they are worth to either of us.

## What to check before you approve

Three things catch most problems. Check the internal dimensions against a real build, not
against the number you remember. Check the artwork at the size it will actually print,
because a logo that looks fine on screen can lose detail on uncoated board. And check the
finish against the board — the trade-offs are in our guide to
[choosing a finish for printed boxes](/gloss-matte-or-spot-uv-burger-boxes/).

## Reordering

Once a run is printed the specification and artwork are held, so a repeat order is a quantity
and a date rather than a fresh brief. That is also the natural point to extend the run
length: if the design has proven itself, ordering more at once is the cheapest change you can
make to your unit price.

## Starting

Send the size, the style and the quantity. If you only have two of the three, send those and
we will work the third out with you — the [wholesale quote form](/wholesale-burger-boxes/)
sets out what to include.
