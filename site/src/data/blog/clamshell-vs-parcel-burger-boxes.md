Most burger packaging decisions come down to these two shapes. They cost about the same and
hold the same burger, but they behave differently everywhere it matters.

## The clamshell

A clamshell hinges at the back and closes with a tuck or a tab at the front. One motion
opens it, one closes it, and there is nothing to assemble. In a busy service that speed is
the whole argument: the box comes off the shelf ready, the burger goes in, the lid comes
down.

It also opens flat, which means the customer can eat out of the base without holding the
lid out of the way. For counter service and dine-in, that is usually the right shape.

Its weakness is depth. A clamshell is designed around a single burger, and a very tall
stacked build either does not fit or arrives compressed.

## The parcel box

A parcel or takeaway box is squarer and deeper, closing over the top rather than folding
back. It handles taller builds, it holds a burger with a side alongside it, and its shape
stacks more predictably — which matters when four orders go into one delivery bag and the
bottom one carries the weight.

The cost is a step in service. A deeper box takes slightly longer to close, and it does not
open flat, so eating directly from it is less comfortable.

Both styles sit alongside window, foldable and tray formats under
[burger box styles by type](/product-category/boxes-by-type/).

## Choosing by how you hand food over

If most orders are eaten on site or carried a short distance, the clamshell is faster in
service and better to eat from. If most orders go into a bag with other orders and travel,
the parcel box protects the build better and stacks without leaning.

Plenty of operations use both — clamshells over the counter and parcel boxes for delivery —
and because runs are printed to order, the artwork can be identical across the two so the
brand does not change depending on how the customer ordered.

## Where board matters more than shape

Whichever shape you pick, the board decides whether it survives the journey. A clamshell in
corrugated board will outlast a parcel box in thin cardboard on a long delivery. If your
orders travel, choose the board first and the shape second — the trade-offs are set out
under [burger boxes by material](/product-category/boxes-by-material/).

## Size still decides the outcome

Neither shape rescues a badly sized box. A clamshell with too much headroom lets the burger
slide just as a parcel box does, and a parcel box that is too tight crushes the crown of the
bun just as a clamshell would. Measure the build before choosing the style — the method is
in our guide to
[choosing a burger box size](/how-to-choose-a-burger-box-size/).
