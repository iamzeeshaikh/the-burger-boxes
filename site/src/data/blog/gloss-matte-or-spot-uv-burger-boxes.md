Finish is the cheapest way to change how packaging feels in the hand, and the easiest place
to spend money without getting anything back. Three options cover almost every burger box.

## Gloss

Gloss puts a reflective layer over the print. Colors come up brighter and deeper, photographs
look better, and the surface wipes clean rather than absorbing a smear. It is the safe choice
for artwork that leans on color — bright brand palettes, food photography, anything that
should look vivid under a counter light.

The trade-off is that gloss shows fingerprints and scuffs, and on a large flat area it can
read as cheap if the print underneath is not sharp.

## Matte

Matte scatters light instead of reflecting it. Colors sit flatter and slightly darker, edges
look softer, and the surface feels closer to the board. It reads as more restrained, which
is why it suits brands built around simplicity, and it hides handling marks far better than
gloss does.

If your artwork is mostly type and a logo rather than photography, matte usually looks more
expensive than gloss for the same money.

## Spot UV

Spot UV is not an alternative to the other two — it goes on top of them. A high-gloss layer
is applied to selected areas only, so a logo or a pattern catches the light against a matte
background. Done sparingly it is the single most effective way to make a box feel
considered; done across too much of the box it stops being a contrast and becomes another
gloss finish.

The rule of thumb: pick one element. A logo, a wordmark, or a single graphic. If you are
spot-varnishing three things, none of them stands out.

## What the finish does not fix

A coating is a surface treatment, not a structural one. It will not stop a thin box crushing
in a delivery bag, and while it slows grease at the surface it is not a substitute for a
liner where the build is wet. Board choice handles the first — see
[burger boxes by material](/product-category/boxes-by-material/) — and a barrier inside the
box handles the second.

## Board changes the result

The same finish behaves differently on different stock. White coated board gives the truest
color and takes gloss best. Kraft mutes everything and shifts it warm, and gloss on kraft
tends to look uneven, which is why matte or no coating usually suits brown board better.
Decide the board and the finish together rather than one after the other.

## A practical starting point
